Ser vs. Estar

After this lesson you can choose correctly between the two Spanish verbs for "to be" — and avoid the classic mistakes that change the meaning of a sentence.

The idea

English has one verb "to be". Spanish has two: ser and estar. A useful first rule of thumb:

  • Ser — what something is: its identity and essential characteristics.
  • Estar — how or where something is: its state, condition or location.

Watch the accents: está (is) vs. esta (this). The accent changes both the word and where the stress falls: es-TÁ vs. ES-ta.

When to use SER — remember "DOCTOR"

UseExampleMeaning
DescriptionElla es alta.She is tall.
OccupationSoy estudiante.I am a student.
CharacteristicMi amigo es simpático.My friend is nice.
Time & dateSon las tres.It is three o'clock.
OriginSomos de Chile.We are from Chile.
RelationshipAna es mi hermana.Ana is my sister.

Ser is also used for what something is made of (La mesa es de madera — the table is made of wood) and for where an event takes place (see the traps below).

When to use ESTAR — remember "PLACE"

UseExampleMeaning
PositionEl gato está sentado.The cat is sitting.
LocationLa biblioteca está cerca.The library is nearby.
Action (in progress)Estoy estudiando.I am studying.
ConditionLa puerta está abierta.The door is open.
EmotionEstamos contentos.We are happy.

Three classic traps

1. Events use SER for location. Things and people are located with estar, but events take place — so they use ser.

La fiesta es en mi casa. — The party is at my house.
Mi casa está en el centro. — My house is downtown.

2. Death uses ESTAR. Even though it is permanent, "dead" is grammatically a state:

El pez está muerto. — The fish is dead.

3. "Permanent vs. temporary" is only a rough guide. Time is temporary but uses ser (Son las tres); location can be permanent but uses estar (Madrid está en España). When in doubt, check DOCTOR and PLACE.

Same adjective, different meaning

Some adjectives change meaning depending on the verb. Ser gives the trait; estar gives the state.

Practice

Choose ser or estar and conjugate it. Then open the answer.

1. Mis padres ___ de Colombia.

son — origin (ser). "My parents are from Colombia."

2. ¿Dónde ___ el baño?

está — location of a thing (estar). "Where is the bathroom?"

3. El concierto ___ en el estadio.

es — location of an event (ser). "The concert is at the stadium."

4. Yo ___ cansado después del examen.

estoy — condition (estar). "I am tired after the exam."

5. ___ el 15 de octubre.

Es — date (ser). "It is October 15th."

6. Nosotros ___ viendo una película.

estamos — action in progress (estar + -ndo). "We are watching a movie."

Solving Quadratic Equations

After this lesson you can solve any quadratic equation, pick the fastest method, and predict the kind of solutions before you start.

The idea

A quadratic equation is any equation that can be written in standard form:

$$ ax^2 + bx + c = 0, \qquad a \neq 0 $$

The solutions (also called roots) are the values of \( x \) that make the equation true. Graphically, they are where the parabola \( y = ax^2 + bx + c \) crosses the \( x \)-axis. A quadratic has at most two real roots.

Always start here: move everything to one side so the equation equals 0, then read off \( a \), \( b \) and \( c \). For \( 3x^2 = 5 - 2x \), rewrite as \( 3x^2 + 2x - 5 = 0 \), so \( a = 3,\ b = 2,\ c = -5 \).

Method 1 — Factoring

If a product equals zero, at least one factor must be zero (the zero-product rule). So if you can write the quadratic as two brackets, you are almost done.

Example: solve \( x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0 \).

Find two numbers that multiply to \( c = 6 \) and add to \( b = -5 \): that's \( -2 \) and \( -3 \).

$$ (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2 \text{ or } x = 3 $$

Check \( x = 2 \): \( 4 - 10 + 6 = 0 \) ✅

Common mistake: never divide both sides by \( x \). In \( x^2 = 5x \), dividing gives only \( x = 5 \) and loses \( x = 0 \). Instead factor: \( x^2 - 5x = x(x - 5) = 0 \), so \( x = 0 \) or \( x = 5 \).

Method 2 — Square roots (when \( b = 0 \))

If there is no \( x \) term, isolate \( x^2 \) and take the square root — remember both signs.

Example: solve \( 2x^2 - 18 = 0 \).

$$ 2x^2 = 18 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 = 9 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm 3 $$

Method 3 — Completing the square

Rewrite the left side as a perfect square \( (x + k)^2 \). The trick: with \( a = 1 \), add \( \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 \) to both sides.

Example: solve \( x^2 + 6x - 7 = 0 \). Here \( \left(\tfrac{6}{2}\right)^2 = 9 \), so add 9 to both sides.

$$ \begin{aligned} x^2 + 6x &= 7 \\ x^2 + 6x + 9 &= 7 + 9 \\ (x + 3)^2 &= 16 \\ x + 3 &= \pm 4 \\ x &= 1 \text{ or } x = -7 \end{aligned} $$

This method is also how the quadratic formula is derived (see below).

Method 4 — The quadratic formula (always works)

$$ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} $$

Example: solve \( 2x^2 + 3x - 2 = 0 \). Here \( a = 2,\ b = 3,\ c = -2 \).

$$ \begin{aligned} x &= \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{3^2 - 4(2)(-2)}}{2(2)} \\[4pt] &= \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4} = \frac{-3 \pm 5}{4} \\[4pt] x &= \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x = -2 \end{aligned} $$

Check \( x = \tfrac12 \): \( 2\cdot\tfrac14 + \tfrac32 - 2 = \tfrac12 + \tfrac32 - 2 = 0 \) ✅

Where does the formula come from? (derivation)

Complete the square on the general equation:

$$ \begin{aligned} ax^2 + bx + c &= 0 \\ x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x &= -\frac{c}{a} \\ x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{b^2}{4a^2} &= \frac{b^2}{4a^2} - \frac{c}{a} \\ \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 &= \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2} \\ x + \frac{b}{2a} &= \pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \\ x &= \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \end{aligned} $$

The discriminant: know the answer type first

The expression under the square root, \( \Delta = b^2 - 4ac \), is called the discriminant.

DiscriminantSolutionsExample
\( \Delta > 0 \)Two different real roots\( x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0 \): \( \Delta = 1 \), roots 2 and 3
\( \Delta = 0 \)One repeated real root\( x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0 \): \( \Delta = 0 \), root 3
\( \Delta < 0 \)No real roots (two complex roots)\( x^2 + 2x + 5 = 0 \): \( \Delta = -16 \)

For the last example, the formula still works using \( \sqrt{-16} = 4i \):

$$ x = \frac{-2 \pm 4i}{2} = -1 \pm 2i $$

Quick check (Vieta's formulas): the two roots always satisfy \( x_1 + x_2 = -\tfrac{b}{a} \) and \( x_1 \cdot x_2 = \tfrac{c}{a} \). For \( 2x^2 + 3x - 2 = 0 \): \( \tfrac12 + (-2) = -\tfrac32 \) ✅ and \( \tfrac12 \cdot (-2) = -1 \) ✅

Which method should I use?

The equation looks like…Use
No \( x \) term (\( b = 0 \))Square roots
No constant (\( c = 0 \))Factor out \( x \)
Small whole numbers that factor nicelyFactoring
Anything else, or you're unsureQuadratic formula

Practice

Try each one on paper first, then open the solution.

1. \( x^2 + x - 12 = 0 \)

Numbers that multiply to \( -12 \) and add to \( 1 \): \( 4 \) and \( -3 \). $$ (x + 4)(x - 3) = 0 $$ So \( x = -4 \) or \( x = 3 \).

2. \( 3x^2 - 12x = 0 \)

No constant, so factor out \( 3x \): $$ 3x(x - 4) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 0 \text{ or } x = 4 $$

3. \( x^2 - 4x - 1 = 0 \)

Doesn't factor nicely, so use the formula with \( a = 1,\ b = -4,\ c = -1 \): $$ \begin{aligned} x &= \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 4}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} \\[4pt] &= 2 \pm \sqrt{5} \end{aligned} $$

4. \( 4x^2 + 4x + 1 = 0 \)

\( \Delta = 16 - 16 = 0 \), so one repeated root. It's a perfect square: $$ (2x + 1)^2 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = -\tfrac{1}{2} $$

5. \( x^2 + 4x + 13 = 0 \)

\( \Delta = 16 - 52 = -36 < 0 \), so no real roots. Complex roots: $$ \begin{aligned} x &= \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{-36}}{2} = \frac{-4 \pm 6i}{2} \\[4pt] &= -2 \pm 3i \end{aligned} $$

Quiz

1. How many real solutions does \( x^2 - 4x + 4 = 0 \) have?

2. Solve \( x^2 - 9 = 0 \).

3. What are the solutions of \( (x - 5)(x + 2) = 0 \)?

4. What is the discriminant of \( 2x^2 + 3x - 5 = 0 \)?

5. To complete the square for \( x^2 + 8x \), what number do you add?

6. Solve \( x^2 = 5x \).

7. Without solving, what is the sum of the roots of \( 3x^2 - 12x + 7 = 0 \)?

8. Solve \( x^2 + 2x + 10 = 0 \).

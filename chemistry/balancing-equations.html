Balancing Chemical Equations

After this lesson you can balance any basic chemical equation with a reliable step-by-step method.

The idea

In a chemical reaction, atoms are never created or destroyed — they are only rearranged. This is the law of conservation of mass. So every type of atom must appear the same number of times on both sides of the arrow.

An unbalanced equation for making water looks like this:

$$ \ce{H2 + O2 -> H2O} $$

Count the oxygen: 2 atoms on the left, only 1 on the right. Something is missing. We fix it by placing coefficients (big numbers in front of formulas):

$$ \ce{2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O} $$

Now: hydrogen 4 = 4, oxygen 2 = 2. Balanced.

Golden rule: change only the coefficients, never the small subscripts. Changing \( \ce{H2O} \) to \( \ce{H2O2} \) turns water into hydrogen peroxide — a different substance!

The method

  1. Write the unbalanced equation with correct formulas.
  2. Count the atoms of each element on both sides.
  3. Balance elements that appear in only one compound on each side first (often metals or carbon).
  4. Leave hydrogen and oxygen — and any element standing alone, like \( \ce{O2} \) — for last.
  5. Recount everything. Use the smallest whole-number coefficients.

Tip: If a polyatomic ion like \( \ce{SO4^2-} \) appears unchanged on both sides, balance it as a single unit instead of atom by atom.

Worked example: burning propane

$$ \ce{C3H8 + O2 -> CO2 + H2O} $$

Step 1 — carbon. 3 C on the left, so put 3 in front of \( \ce{CO2} \):

$$ \ce{C3H8 + O2 -> 3CO2 + H2O} $$

Step 2 — hydrogen. 8 H on the left; each water has 2 H, so we need 4 waters:

$$ \ce{C3H8 + O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O} $$

Step 3 — oxygen (last, because \( \ce{O2} \) stands alone). Right side: \( 3 \times 2 + 4 \times 1 = 10 \) oxygen atoms. Each \( \ce{O2} \) gives 2, so we need 5:

$$ \ce{C3H8 + 5O2 -> 3CO2 + 4H2O} $$

Step 4 — check.

ElementLeftRight
C33
H84 × 2 = 8
O5 × 2 = 103 × 2 + 4 = 10

Balanced. ✅

Practice

Try each one on paper first, then open the solution.

\( \ce{N2 + H2 -> NH3} \)

$$ \ce{N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3} $$ N: 2 = 2. H: 6 = 6. (Nitrogen first: 2 NH₃; then hydrogen: 6 H needs 3 H₂.)

\( \ce{Fe + O2 -> Fe2O3} \)

$$ \ce{4Fe + 3O2 -> 2Fe2O3} $$ Oxygen 2 on the left and 3 on the right: the lowest common multiple is 6, so 3 O₂ and 2 Fe₂O₃. That gives 4 Fe on the right, so 4 Fe on the left.

\( \ce{Al + HCl -> AlCl3 + H2} \)

$$ \ce{2Al + 6HCl -> 2AlCl3 + 3H2} $$ Cl: 6 = 6. H: 6 = 6. Al: 2 = 2.
